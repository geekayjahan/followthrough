# Kāma workflow map

Source of record for what the business actually runs on. Built 29 Oct 2026 from Zoho Mail (Kāma folders, sent, drafts), Zoho Books (43 invoices, 72+ expenses, contacts, items), Google Calendar, Drive (Roaster Pipeline sheet, import/customs checklist, brand docs, Followthrough brief). Anything inferred rather than seen is marked **ASSUMED**. Names, companies, emails, phone numbers and person-linked amounts are replaced with roles.

Roles used: owner (GK), partner (helps with transport), farm supplier (the estate in India and its lab), freight agent, customs broker, haulier, warehouse, roasting partner, roaster lead, roaster customer, café customer, retail customer, stall customer, market organiser, courier, accountant (ASSUMED exists), the digest (the existing pipeline script).

Time per week is the owner's current manual time, estimated from message volume and dates. All time figures are **ASSUMED** unless stated.

## D. Things the app must never do on its own

- Send any message to a customer, lead, supplier, agent or organiser. Every outbound needs the owner's approval, each time, with the final text shown.
- Move money: no bank transfer, no FX conversion, no bill payment, no refund, no payment link creation. Books writes limited to expenses, customer payments, sales, items.
- Confirm, hold or allocate a lot to a roaster. It may propose; the owner commits.
- Quote a price. It may draft from the approved price list; a draft with a placeholder or a price outside the list is blocked.
- Release goods from the warehouse or book haulage.
- Pay or commit to the estate (PI, pre-booking).
- Send anything to a declined organisation or its domain.
- Send from any alias other than the coffee alias when the counterpart is a coffee contact.
- Email or message a real person while APP_LIVE is unset; test lists only.
- Delete or merge a contact, invoice or expense.
- Post publicly (Instagram, website, Shopify listing changes).
- Send anything to HMRC or Companies House, or sign a filing.
- Write a stock movement without a source event (order, sale, sample, stall cash-up) it can cite.
- Mark an invoice paid without a matching bank line.
- Re-chase after the owner said "hold" or the counterpart said "not now".

## E. Open questions

## F. Blind spots and nightmares (not asked for by name, found in the data)

1. **You have oversold two lots.** Your estate order asked for 109 kg Yeast Maceration and 35 kg Vintage 24; the estate holds 94 kg and 21 kg. Roasters have been told bags are held. The ledger that would have caught this does not exist.
2. **The cash sandwich.** Roasters pay you by a Monday; you pay the estate after; the estate blocks lots meanwhile. One roaster paying late, or one pulling out after you have paid in INR, leaves you holding stock priced near cost with FX exposure and no deposit.
3. **The one-person shipment.** If you are ill the week a container lands, nobody else knows the broker, the UCN, the haulier, the warehouse contact or which roaster gets which bag. April's clearance took two days of your chasing on one reference.
4. **Samples age in a garage.** A roaster scored a 6-month-old roast as past its prime and told you so. Every stale sample costs a lead and the estate's reputation.
5. **Four businesses, one inbox, one Books org.** A roaster negotiation went out from the consulting domain. Two invoice number series exist. Consulting course sales and coffee sit in the same turnover for VAT threshold purposes.
6. **Zero chase on the only overdue invoice.** 74 days, no reminder, while you chase ten roasters by hand for feedback. The system chases leads and ignores debtors.
7. **Pre-orders with no paper.** Zero sales orders, zero estimates in Books. Every allocation lives in an email thread, with no terms, no incoterms, no who-pays-haulage, no cancellation rule.
8. **Cold email to sole traders.** Several leads are on free-mail addresses, which PECR treats as individuals needing consent. One complaint to the ICO is a bigger problem than a declined lead.
9. **Books are reconstructed, not kept.** Mileage from a stated weekly average, receipts marked missing, subsistence vs entertaining unresolved. Fine until an HMRC enquiry or a loan application.
10. **Transport is a favour economy.** No car on a market day meant 15 emails to strangers and a taxi. Partner's work travel decides when a roaster gets their sacks.
11. **The digest can email real roasters.** Its own test output warns every recipient must be on the test list. One config slip sends placeholder-laden drafts to the people you are courting.
12. **Compliance lapses are silent.** PLI expiry, food registration, confirmation statement, domain renewals: nothing watches the dates except email reminders in a notification folder you do not read.
13. **Pricing under pressure.** You wrote that a large-roaster price "can still drop to a loss" on fuel or currency. There is no landed-cost sheet to say where the floor is.
14. **Seasonal peak missed.** A two-day Christmas market was cancelled for lack of stock because roasting batches are booked reactively.
15. **Reputation data lives in your head.** Which roaster cupped what, scored what, asked what, promised what. The estate asks you for this; you reconstruct it from threads.
16. **Hackathon and course calendar vs harvest.** Your calendar for Nov to Dec is full of non-coffee sessions exactly when the next shipment lands and the estate wants pre-bookings.
