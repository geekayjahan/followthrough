1 · Problem

Who. Owner-operators of small B2B businesses where a sale takes more than one conversation and relationships run through chat and email: 1 to 20 people, no sales or finance staff. User one is GK, solo owner of a small business importing Indian green coffee for UK roasters and a market stall.

Pain now. The work is a chain of open loops (a lead, a sample out, a quote, an order, an invoice, a receipt, a lot of stock, a stall day) scattered across mail, the accounting tool, spreadsheets, Instagram and the owner's head. Nothing flags which loop is going cold, so leads aren't chased, invoices go late and overdue ones aren't chased, receipts never reach the books, stock is unknown until it runs out, orders from chat and stall don't land in one place, stall days aren't cashed up, and the owner can't see cash, owed, stock or what's due. New leads get a blanket approach: nothing checks whether they even sell specialty coffee, what tier they sell at, or whether they already know an existing customer, supplier or stockist, so warm paths are pitched cold and the wrong leads get the owner's time. The owner is the data controller for every lead and customer under UK GDPR, and the current tools give no answer to where a person's data sits, how long it is kept, or how it would be erased.

Outcome that counts, in order. (0) No real person's data is ever handled, and going live would be lawful. (1) Nothing leaks: every loop gets its next move on time without the owner remembering; the owner only decides and approves. (2) Every new lead arrives qualified, tiered and placed in the owner's network before the first message is drafted. (3) At any moment, on any device, the owner can see cash, owed, stock, due today and the background on any lead, and it is right. (4) All of it under an hour a day, from the phone.

2 · What good looks like

One engine, not five apps. Everything the owner must not forget is a loop: an open item with a type, a counterpart, a last-touch time and a next move. The app keeps every loop, notices which has gone cold, drafts the next move, and asks the owner to approve. Nothing reaches a customer without a tap or a voice note from the owner. 2.1 Qualify, tier, place

A lead's website and public Instagram profile are read once. The bar is retail price per 250g: under £8 fails and the lead is closed with the reason recorded; £8 to £12 is mid-shelf; above £12 is premium. Whether Indian coffee is already on the shelf is recorded. The lead is then placed in the owner's network: a graph of organisations (roasters, cafes, suppliers, stockists) and the edges between them (buys from, supplies, stocks, knows). A lead with a path to an existing customer, supplier or stockist ranks above a stranger in the brief; its first draft names the shared connection instead of a template; and the brief proposes who could make the introduction, with a draft to them. The web page draws the map and, for any lead, an expandable background panel: tier, price, Indian coffee on shelf, path to the network, source of each fact.

2.2 Surfaces and primary action

WhatsApp is where the owner meets the app: a brief at 08:00 and 18:00 Europe/London, nothing unprompted in between. The primary action is approve: a tap, a reply, or a voice note, which is transcribed and acted on. One web page, "Where I stand": cash in Books, who owes what, kg left per lot, loops due today, the network map, the lead background panel. Read-only except approvals.

2.3 Data boundary and GDPR by design

No real person's data enters the app. Leads, customers, suppliers and their relationships come from a synthetic file the owners write (schema in §3); the Zoho Mail and Zoho Books accounts are fresh ones created for this app; every email address in the data is under example.test and every WhatsApp recipient is on the Cloud API test list. The spec carries the panel's answers: data map (which class of data sits in Zoho, the app store, WhatsApp, the model host; nothing persists on the model host), lawful basis for live use (legitimate interests for corporate B2B contacts under UK GDPR and PECR, to be verified against ICO guidance before any live send), retention (12 months from last touch, Next), erasure on request within 30 days (Next), and a suppression list: a counterpart who asks to stop is never drafted to again, and the block covers their organisation's domain (Core).

3 · Inputs & interfaces

  • Synthetic dataset at app/data/synthetic.json, written by the owners, validated by the build at start. Collections: orgs (id, name, type roaster|cafe|supplier|stockist, website, instagram, city, price_per_250g, sells_indian, status lead|customer|declined), people (id, org, name, email@example.test, whatsapp test number or null, role), edges (from, to, kind buys_from|supplies|stocks|knows, since, note), invoices (id, org, amount, issued, due, paid_on), lots (id, name, start_kg, left_kg), orders (id, org, lot, kg, taken_via, status), stall_days (date, cashed_up).
  • Zoho Mail, fresh account (TBC, owner GK): read threads, create drafts, send. Zoho Books, fresh organisation (TBC): read contacts, invoices, payments, expenses; write expenses, customer payments, sales and items. OAuth refresh token in app/.env.
  • WhatsApp Cloud API, Meta test number with up to 5 verified recipients; permanent system-user token in app/.env; webhook for inbound messages and voice notes.
  • Lead websites (shop pages) and public Instagram profiles, read once per lead for enrichment; access path is an open stack row.
  • Owner voice notes via WhatsApp, transcribed by the Meta voice model in the stack.

  • Landed cost, customs and duty per lot; courier booking and delivery tracking (Next).
  • Instagram DMs as a customer channel; warm intros scraped from Instagram follower graphs (Later).
  • Helpers, co-runner, second business, roles beyond one shared owner login (Later).

  • Ask on WhatsApp, Glimmer local mode, a real WhatsApp Business number (Next).

5 · Verification

Written as checks in app/evals.md, run at S3. Evidence for every check comes from one of: the action log app/logs/actions.jsonl (every brief, approval, send, Books write, with timestamps), the fresh Zoho Books records, the WhatsApp test-list inbox, or the web page. Happy path: F1 cold lead drafted in the brief · F2 tap approval sends on the right channel · F3 voice-note approval · F4 overdue invoice chased · F5 receipt becomes expense · F6 stall voice note becomes sale and stock movement · F7 low lot opens a loop · F8 lead qualified and tiered · F9 warm path ranks first and names the connection · F10 introducer proposed · F11 map and background panel · F12 web page is right. Edges: E1 soft no ends the thread, no decline · E2 payment closes the invoice loop · E3 brief sent exactly at 08:00 and 18:00, nothing between · E4 lead below the bar is closed with reason. Adversarial: A1 unapproved message never leaves · A2 nothing reaches a non-test recipient before live · A3 declined domain never drafted · A4 no payment or bank write exists · A5 secret scan of the public repo passes · A6 no address outside example.test and no non-test WhatsApp number in the data.

7 · Ideas for the build

  • A demo clock: an env switch that ages loops by 3 days so a judge sees a brief in 30 seconds, not 3 days.
  • Warm path as a sentence in the brief ("Bean & Bloom stock Harbour Roasters, who buy lot 2"), not a score.
  • Approve by emoji reaction on the brief message; edit by quoting the draft with a change.
  • A weekly "what leaked" count on the web page: loops that went cold twice. This is the hackathon's impact metric.
  • The lead background panel shows the source line for each fact (which page, which post), so the panel can defend enrichment.
  • Voice-note cash-up reads back what it heard before asking for approval.
